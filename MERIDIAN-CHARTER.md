# Meridian --- Product Charter

**Document ID:** MER-CHARTER-001\
**Version:** 0.1\
**Status:** Working baseline --- pending founder approval\
**Product:** Meridian\
**Parent organisation:** Digital Pitcher Networks (DPN)\
**Created:** 10 November 2026

> This Charter establishes Meridian's product identity, purpose,
> intended customer, guiding commitments and scope boundaries. It is the
> product-level authority for subsequent specifications and planning. It
> does not describe or certify the current implementation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Product identity

**Meridian is a founder operating system: a coherent digital workspace
that helps founders organise, understand and coordinate the essential
work of building and running a business.**

It brings together the operational context that is often fragmented
across separate tools, files, messages, spreadsheets and personal
systems.

Meridian is intended to provide a dependable operating environment in
which a founder can see what is happening, understand what needs
attention, preserve important business context and move work forward.

Meridian is an independent product brand and an incubated venture within
Digital Pitcher Networks (DPN).

## 2. Purpose

Meridian exists to reduce the operational fragmentation experienced by
founders.

A founder should not have to reconstruct the state of their business
from disconnected task lists, calendars, documents, financial records,
conversations and notes every time they need to make a decision or take
action.

Meridian aims to bring the relevant parts of that operating picture into
one structured environment.

Its purpose is not to replace every specialist tool. It is to provide a
useful, coherent layer for organising the founder's work and business
context, while integrating with reliable external tools where that is
more sensible than rebuilding their capabilities.

## 3. The problem Meridian addresses

Founders commonly operate across multiple systems:

-   Work and projects are tracked in one place.
-   Deadlines and commitments live in calendars and messages.
-   Financial information is spread across spreadsheets, exports and
    accounting tools.
-   Contacts and business relationships are stored in different address
    books and services.
-   Important decisions remain buried in conversations.
-   Documents are distributed across drives, devices and email.
-   Progress and priorities must be manually pieced together.

The resulting problems include fragmented context, repeated
administration, weak continuity, missed commitments and difficulty
maintaining a clear view of the business.

Meridian should address this fragmentation through structure, useful
connections and clear presentation---not by creating another layer of
unnecessary administration.

## 4. Initial customer

The initial customer is the **solo founder**.

The first product should be useful to someone building or operating a
business without requiring a large team, a complex enterprise setup or a
dedicated operations department.

The initial deployment direction is self-deployed: the founder sets up
and uses their own Meridian workspace.

This initial focus is a deliberate starting point. It does not
permanently restrict Meridian's future audience, but expansion into
teams, agencies, enterprises or other customer groups must be supported
by explicit product decisions and validated needs.

## 5. Product promise

Meridian should help a founder:

1.  Understand the current state of their work and business.
2.  Organise projects, tasks, milestones and commitments.
3.  Keep important contacts, decisions and documents accessible.
4.  Record and review relevant financial information.
5.  Maintain continuity across day-to-day operations.
6.  Identify priorities and upcoming obligations.
7.  Build a more coherent operational picture without adopting an
    unnecessarily complex system.

Meridian should make the founder feel more organised and in
control---not monitored, overwhelmed or dependent on opaque automation.

## 6. Product character

Meridian should embody the qualities of a dependable, discreet and
capable operating companion.

Its intended character is:

-   **Dependable:** predictable, consistent and trustworthy.
-   **Observant:** able to surface relevant context and signals.
-   **Discreet:** careful with sensitive business information.
-   **Composed:** calm and clear rather than noisy or demanding.
-   **Practical:** focused on useful outcomes rather than novelty.
-   **Supportive:** helps the founder exercise better judgement without
    taking away agency.

The product's character may draw inspiration from the qualities of an
excellent trusted steward. It should not imitate a fictional character
or adopt that character's identity.

## 7. Core product domains

The initial product direction includes the following operating domains:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Domain                              Responsibility
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Command Centre / Home               Provide an overview of current
                                      activity, priorities and relevant
                                      signals. Final naming and screen
                                      composition should follow the
                                      approved design specification.

  Projects                            Organise initiatives, workstreams,
                                      outcomes and project context.

  Tasks                               Manage actionable work, status and
                                      due dates.

  Calendar & Milestones               Organise dates, milestones and
                                      commitments.

  Finances                            Record and review financial
                                      information, initially through
                                      import.

  Contacts                            Maintain useful records of people
                                      and business relationships.

  Decisions                           Preserve important decisions and
                                      their rationale.

  Vault / Documents                   Store and organise important
                                      business documents.

  Reports                             Present useful summaries and
                                      operational visibility.

  Settings                            Manage workspace and product
                                      preferences.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

These domains establish the intended product surface. Their detailed
behaviour, data structures, dependencies and acceptance criteria must be
defined in specifications and checked against the existing application.

## 8. Product commitments

Meridian commits to the following:

### 8.1 One coherent workspace

The initial product uses a single workspace. Its modules should share a
coherent experience and consistent operating logic.

### 8.2 Clarity over feature volume

A feature should solve a recognisable problem. Meridian should not
accumulate features merely to appear comprehensive.

### 8.3 User control

The founder remains in control of important decisions, data connections
and consequential actions. The product should explain what it does and
avoid opaque behaviour.

### 8.4 Trust and discretion

Business information must be handled carefully. Access, permissions,
storage and data movement should be understandable and appropriately
protected.

### 8.5 Practical integration

Use established external services where they provide reliable
capabilities and integration is preferable to building and maintaining a
replacement.

### 8.6 Honest operational visibility

The product should distinguish recorded facts, imported information,
estimates, suggestions and incomplete data. It must not imply certainty
where the underlying information is incomplete.

### 8.7 Deliberate extensibility

The core should support future expansion without forcing every possible
industry workflow or advanced capability into the initial product.

## 9. Initial scope boundaries

The initial product is focused on a solo founder's essential operating
environment.

The following are not part of the established V1 baseline unless
explicitly approved later:

-   Multi-workspace operation.
-   Full team collaboration and complex role management.
-   Autonomous AI agents or an agent marketplace.
-   Full CRM replacement.
-   Direct banking and accounting integrations.
-   Native mobile applications.
-   Broad autonomous automation.
-   Mandatory industry-specific operational modules.
-   A complex enterprise administration layer.

These capabilities may be reconsidered when evidence, customer needs and
available capacity justify them. Their presence in future discussions
does not make them current requirements.

The name **Jared** is reserved as a possible name for a future AI agent
within Meridian. This reservation does not approve the agent concept,
its scope or its inclusion in V1.

## 10. Industry adaptability

Meridian's general-purpose foundation should be useful across different
kinds of businesses.

Potential industry extensions have been explored for small-scale
distilleries and creative or development agencies. These may eventually
become optional industry packs, with specialised workflows built on top
of shared core capabilities.

The core product should not become tied to one industry without a
deliberate strategic decision.

Industry packs are exploratory until separately researched, specified
and approved.

## 11. Relationship with DPN

DPN is the parent venture-building organisation. Meridian is an
independently branded venture incubated within DPN.

The established organisational default is that DPN owns and operates the
ventures it creates, subject to applicable law and properly documented
agreements.

This Charter records product and organisational intent. It is not proof
of legal ownership, an intellectual-property assignment or a substitute
for contracts and legal advice.

Meridian retains its own product identity, product direction, roadmap,
architecture and documentation. DPN-level strategy and governance should
be referenced where relevant without turning Meridian's product
documentation into a duplicate of DPN's organisational records.

## 12. Product decision authority

The founder is the approval authority for material changes to Meridian's
product direction and scope.

Contributors and AI agents may research, analyse and recommend changes.
They must not silently convert recommendations into approved
requirements.

A material product decision should identify:

-   The decision being made.
-   The problem or opportunity behind it.
-   The options considered where relevant.
-   The rationale.
-   The impact on scope, users, architecture, cost or existing
    commitments.
-   The relevant documents that must be updated.

Changes to locked product commitments require an explicit decision and
corresponding documentation updates.

## 14. Review and amendment

This Charter is a living product authority, but it should not change
casually.

Review it when:

-   Meridian's target customer or core purpose materially changes.
-   A major scope boundary is reconsidered.
-   The product's relationship with DPN changes.
-   A new strategic direction changes the product's fundamental
    commitments.
-   Experience from real use demonstrates that an established commitment
    is no longer appropriate.

Record amendments with a version, date, summary and approval status.
Preserve superseded versions or revision history in the repository.

## 15. Current approval state

This version is a **working baseline pending founder review**. Its
contents should not be treated as fully locked until the founder
explicitly approves it.

The product decisions summarised here should also be reconciled with the
existing Meridian repository. A product requirement is not evidence that
the corresponding feature has been implemented.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Guiding principle:** Meridian should give founders a clearer, more
dependable way to operate their businesses. It should earn its place in
the founder's workflow through usefulness, coherence and trust---not
through feature count or unnecessary complexity.
